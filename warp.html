About the model

Midplane. Zw = h(R) sin[φ − φw(R, t)], with h growing linearly beyond 8 kpc and φw = φw,0 + (twist)(R − 12 kpc) + ω t. Stars move on circular orbits in a flat rotation curve of 220 km s⁻¹ and oscillate a little about the midplane.

Defaults. The LPV fit in the paper: h ≈ 0.54 kpc at 16 kpc, twist 14.3 deg kpc⁻¹, ω = 9.5 km s⁻¹ kpc⁻¹. This is an illustration, not a fit to the data. It has no growth term and no far-side constraint, and the outer disc is overpopulated so the warp is visible.

Reading it. The galaxy turns clockwise seen from the north. A star crosses the rising and falling halves of the warp at the rate Ω − ω, so a prograde precession slows its vertical oscillation. In the strip chart the dashed curve is the same star in a warp that stands still.
